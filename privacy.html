Twofold Weather / Privacy

Privacy policy.

Twofold has no account system, advertising, tracking or In-App Purchases. From version 1.13 it sends anonymous usage data to TelemetryDeck, an analytics service, unless you turn that off: a short note for each thing you do in the app and a summary of counts and settings once a day, never your places or your weather (see Usage summary). Twofold does not sell personal data and runs no server of its own. Weather providers, and Apple's service for finding a place name, still receive the information needed to answer each request.

Last updated: 21 November 2026. This revision, for version 1.13, adds the anonymous usage data that Twofold sends to TelemetryDeck as the app is used unless you turn it off, and no longer says that Twofold has no analytics service; the 15 November revision, for version 1.8, named the units you chose for wind and distance among the saved preferences and in what the widgets and the watch are given, added that a widget asks Environment Canada for the alerts of a Canadian city, and took out the Roast opt-in, which is no longer saved or shared (the voice you chose still is); the 13 November revision, for version 1.6, added the names and times of official alerts to what is shared with the widgets, and stated that the past-week record and the chosen voice are kept where the widgets can read them; the 11 November revision named the discoveries, the voice and the choice of temperature scales among what is stored on your device; the 10 November revision removed tips and the rain radar, which Twofold has not offered since version 1.5; the 7 November revision added the airport observations and Canadian alerts in version 1.4. This policy covers the Twofold Weather app for iPhone and iPad, its widgets, its Apple Watch app, and this support website, provided by Abhishek Agrawal.

Data stored on your device

  • Saved places and preferences: city names, region and country, coordinates rounded to two decimal places, time zones, the selected city, list order, the temperature scales you show and their order, the units you chose for wind and distance, the home layout, the order, page, sizes and removed state of the cards on Today and Details, the Briefing's activity list, whether the Week keeps yesterday's row and the Briefing keeps Activities on the page, the current-weather layout, the card size (Detailed or Compact), the focused reading's scale and type and the voice you chose. These are kept in the app's preferences (UserDefaults).
  • Forecast alerts: your alert rules (kind, threshold, city, lead time, on or off), a record of each planned notification's forecast time, the optional official-alert notification setting and the ids of NWS and Environment Canada alerts already notified per city. Each notification's content is the city name, the alert or threshold text and the forecast time, plus the saved place's identifier so a tap can open that city.
  • Saved weather: last-good weather snapshots, stored as JSON in the app's cache. When the app restores them, it ignores snapshots older than 48 hours and snapshots for places no longer saved. This is a freshness filter, not a guarantee that every cached file is deleted on schedule.
  • Past week: for each saved city, up to 14 days of past high and low temperatures and the forecasts for them, plus the high and low Twofold showed you the day before, in the Application Support directory of the App Group container that the app shares with its widgets. They are saved with the city's rounded coordinates and excluded from device backups. Only the app writes them; the Past week widget reads them. Removing a city deletes its history.
  • Widget state: a bounded copy of saved places, the selected city, the temperature scales and their order with the units for wind and distance, the voice you chose, weather for widgets and, from version 1.6, the names and times of official alerts in force, in a local App Group container the app shares with its widgets (see Widgets).
  • HTTP responses: a response cache of up to 64 MiB, shared with the widgets. It can hold weather, alerts and city-search results, including place names and coordinates returned by providers, until cache pruning or app deletion, even after their freshness period ends. Older private cache files can remain after an upgrade.
  • Radar from earlier versions: versions before 1.5 had a rain radar and kept its images in a separate cache. Since version 1.5 Twofold has no radar, makes no radar request and empties that cache when it starts. A setting that records whether you had opened Radar can remain in the app's preferences; nothing uses it.
  • Discoveries: which discoveries you have made and the date you first made each, including the ones for things you do in the app (a page pulled down, the voice's line tapped, a voice chosen) and the hidden places you found, and which voices you have tried. These stay in the app's preferences and are not shared with the widgets or the watch. Which ones you found is never sent anywhere; the usage summary carries only how many, as a number.
  • Use and prompts: the days you opened Twofold with weather on screen (as calendar days, not times or places), your longest run of days in a row, how many visits showed fresh weather for a while, and when Twofold last asked for a rating (with the app version). These stay in the app's preferences, are never sent anywhere, and only decide when to ask.
  • Usage summary: from version 1.13, while Share anonymous usage is on, the counts described under Usage summary, the local calendar days they were counted on and the day the last summary was sent, in the app's preferences. Turning the switch off deletes the counts.
  • Request-limit state: request times, weighted costs and provider cooldowns, shared locally so limits survive relaunches and apply to the app and widgets together. This state does not record city names or search text.

Local diagnostic counters and system logs record provider names, request outcomes and durations, cache use, and errors. Twofold's diagnostic messages do not include city names, coordinates, IP addresses, or full request URLs, and they are not sent to the developer.

The shared weather cache and request-limit files are excluded from device backups and use iOS data protection that allows access after the first unlock following a restart. Twofold has no iCloud sync of its own. Preferences and other eligible app data may be included in ordinary device backups, depending on your Apple and device settings.

Location

Location permission is optional. Twofold asks for it only when you tap a location button (Use my location in the intro or the city list, Add My location or Update my location in Cities, or Allow location under Follow my location), and then requests a single approximate location while the app is in use. While Follow my location is on (the default), each time the app comes to the foreground it requests one approximate location if permission is already granted, adds or updates My location, and opens on it after a cold launch, after 15 minutes away, or when you have travelled 10 km or more. With the setting off, the same check runs only while My location is saved, and the selected city never changes. Twofold never asks for permission on its own and uses location only while the app is open. The app rounds the coordinates to two decimal places (roughly 1 km) before saving them, requesting weather, or asking Apple for the place name. Twofold never requests background location, and widgets never request device location.

You can use the app without location permission by searching for a city. Selecting any city still sends that city's rounded coordinates to weather providers, so denying location access does not stop all location-related requests. Rounding does not make a network request anonymous.

City search

Search text between 3 and 100 characters is sent to Open-Meteo's geocoding service after a short typing pause, before you press Search. The text is part of the request address, so it can appear in the provider's server logs. Twofold keeps no search-history list, but search responses may be cached on the device.

Anonymous usage summary

From version 1.13, Twofold tells TelemetryDeck, an analytics service in Germany, how the app is used: a short note as each thing happens (for example “opened the Week page” or “opened the UV sheet from a card”, with the page and the sheet it happened on), sent in small batches every few seconds while the app is open, and once a day, at most, a summary of the day's counts and your settings. It tells the developer which pages and features are used, which are never found, and where a tap took more than one try. Share anonymous usage, in Twofold's Settings → Help & feedback, is on until you turn it off. Turned off, what was counted is deleted from the device, counting stops and nothing more is sent.

What the notes and the summary hold:

  • What you did, one note each and counted in the summary: visits and their rough length (four bands, from under 10 seconds to over 5 minutes), sections opened, swipes that brought something in, sheets opened and how they were reached, cards moved, resized or removed, Settings pages opened and which settings changed (never their values), refreshes and the refreshes that failed (by kind: a request limit, the network, other), permission prompts and their answers, taps on a link to the iOS Settings app, widget taps by the kind of page they open, and the like. Each is a number up to 999.
  • Your settings as fixed words: the temperature scales, the units for wind and distance, the current-weather layout, the voice, how many cards are on Today, whether forecast alerts and the rain Live Activity are on, whether location and notifications are allowed, how many cities are saved as a band (0, 1, 2 to 3, 4 to 6, 7 or more), which of Twofold's widgets are placed (by kind and size), and the text size as a class, Reduce Motion, Increase Contrast, VoiceOver, light or dark appearance and iPhone or iPad.
  • What TelemetryDeck's software adds to every note and summary: the device model, system version, app version and build, whether the app came from the App Store or TestFlight, the phone's language and region setting, its time zone as an offset from UTC, screen size and orientation, appearance and accessibility settings (text size, Reduce Motion, Bold Text and similar), the hour, weekday and month the summary was made, its own count of the days it has run on this device and when it first ran, and an identifier for this installation. It also sends a short note of its own when a visit begins and, the first time it runs, one for a new installation, with the same details and no counts.

The identifier is made from the one Apple gives an app's developer on this device. TelemetryDeck's software scrambles it on the device before it is sent (a SHA-256 hash), and TelemetryDeck scrambles it again on arrival, so neither TelemetryDeck nor the developer can turn it back into the device's identifier or link it to you. It counts installations, not people. TelemetryDeck says it never stores IP addresses and keeps each summary's time only to the hour; see its privacy policy and privacy FAQ.

What the notes and the summary never hold: a place, a city's name or country, coordinates, the text of a search, a temperature or any other weather value, the time of a reading, an alert's threshold, or anything you typed. The widgets and the Apple Watch app count and send nothing.

Until the summary is sent, its counts are kept in the app's preferences. TelemetryDeck's software keeps notes and summaries it could not send in the app's cache and tries again while the app is open, and keeps a small record of its own in a preferences file on the device.

Services that receive requests

Each service below receives your IP address and normal HTTP request information, such as the request time and address, in addition to what is listed.

Third-party services contacted by Twofold
ServiceWhat it receives
Open-Meteo Rounded coordinates for forecasts and current conditions, and for the past week (its Previous Runs service at previous-runs-api.open-meteo.com, only when you view the Past week card or page, at most about once a day per city); the text you enter in city search. Search text in request addresses may appear in provider logs. Its privacy policy says web-server logs may contain IP addresses and coordinates and are deleted after 90 days.

National Weather Service (NWS) For supported US locations and territories: rounded coordinates, and requests for nearby stations, observations and alerts. See the NWS privacy policy.
Aviation Weather Center (NOAA) For saved places outside NWS coverage, a rectangle about 50 km across around the city's rounded coordinates. The app and widgets use it to find nearby airport observations; the watch does not request it. Replies are cached for 10 minutes. See the NWS privacy policy.
Environment and Climate Change Canada (MSC GeoMet) For saved Canadian cities, a small area around the city's rounded coordinates. The app asks when it refreshes in the foreground and, from version 1.8, a widget asks when it updates; the watch does not request these alerts. Replies are cached for five minutes. See the Government of Canada privacy notice.
TelemetryDeck Only while Share anonymous usage is on: the notes and the daily summary described under Usage summary, with the scrambled installation identifier. TelemetryDeck says it never stores IP addresses. See the TelemetryDeck privacy policy.
Apple Rounded coordinates for finding a place name after a location request. See Apple Maps privacy and Apple's privacy policy.

Weather requests use HTTPS, store no cookies or credentials, and send a fixed app User-Agent that includes the developer's support email. They carry no user or device identifier.

Opening an external link, such as a provider page, uses your browser and that website's own privacy practices.

Widgets and the App Group

The app and its widget extension share a local App Group container on the device. It holds a bounded copy of saved places, the selected city, the temperature scales and their order with the units for wind and distance, the voice you chose, and weather for widgets (up to 48 forecast hours per city, and at most 2 MiB of shared weather). From version 1.6 the copy also holds, for each official alert in force at the last check for a city, the alert's name, its identifier and its start, expiry and end times, and the time of that check; it never holds the bulletin's text. The container also holds the past-week record, which the Past week widget reads. WidgetKit also keeps each widget's city choice and rendered timeline.

Installed widgets can request weather for saved cities while the app is closed, using the same providers, rounded coordinates and request limits. iOS decides when they update. Widget city names and weather are marked privacy-sensitive, so iOS can hide them when the device is locked.

The optional rain Live Activity is drawn on the device from the saved forecast, showing the city's name and its next two hours of precipitation. Nothing new is sent to any provider, and no push service is used.

Apple Watch

If the Twofold watch app is installed, the iPhone sends it your saved places (including My location's rounded coordinates and place name), the selected city, a trimmed copy of each city's weather (current reading, 12 hours and 10 days; no alerts) and your temperature scales and their order with the units for wind and distance, voice, card style and accent. The transfer uses Apple's WatchConnectivity between your own paired devices; no Twofold server is involved. The watch keeps the latest copy in its own App Group container, shared only with its complications, and replaces it on every transfer.

The watch app never asks for or uses location. When the iPhone's copy of the city on screen is more than 45 minutes old, the open watch app requests that city's weather from Open-Meteo (and NWS for US places) itself, using the same rounded coordinates, its own request limits and cache, and no air-quality request. Complications show the selected city's name and weather from the saved copy and make no requests. Deleting the watch app removes its copy.

Notifications are local

Forecast alerts are planned on the device from saved forecasts and scheduled with iOS as local notifications. No server sends them, and alert rules and settings are never sent anywhere. Twofold does not register for push notifications.

Ratings

Twofold may ask once in a while for an App Store rating using Apple's standard prompt, which iOS limits. Ratings and reviews are handled by Apple.

No tracking, ads, or accounts

Twofold has no sign-in, advertising or crash-reporting service, and no tracking across other companies' apps or websites. Its one analytics service is TelemetryDeck, which receives the anonymous notes and summary described above and nothing else. It does not use an advertising identifier and does not sell or share personal data for advertising. Forecast alerts are local notifications; the app does not register for push notifications. Apple may process App Store and device diagnostics under your Apple settings and Apple's policies.

Children

Twofold is a general-audience weather app. It has no accounts and does not knowingly collect personal information from children, or from anyone, on a developer-operated server. The provider requests described above work the same way for every user.

Your controls and deletion

  1. In Cities (tap the city name at the top of the app), swipe a saved city to remove it, or use Edit. This removes the place, its saved snapshot, its past-week history and any alerts set for that city only, and asks widgets to reload. The separate HTTP response cache is not erased immediately.
  2. Delete forecast alerts in Twofold's Settings → Forecast alerts, or turn notifications off in iOS Settings → Notifications → Twofold.
  3. Change display preferences through the pages in Twofold's Settings (the gear at the top right). Edit a widget to choose its city, or remove it through the Home Screen or Lock Screen editor. Change location access in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → Twofold. Turning it off does not delete saved places or stop weather requests for them.
  4. Turn off Share anonymous usage in Twofold's Settings → Help & feedback to stop the notes and the summary. The counts on the device are deleted at once, and notes not yet sent are deleted. What was already sent stays with TelemetryDeck, which cannot tell which of them came from you.
  5. To remove all local preferences, alerts, snapshots, past-week history, widget data and caches, delete the app. Offloading the app keeps its data. There is no in-app clear-all control.

Deleting a place or the app does not delete records already held by providers, support emails, or existing device backups. Provider retention follows their linked policies.
