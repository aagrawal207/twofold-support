Using the app

How do I get around the app?

The bar at the bottom holds four sections: Today, Week, Briefing and Details. Tap one, or flick sideways on a section to move to the next one; from Details a flick goes on to Today, and from Today the other way to Details. After dark in the selected city the first section reads Tonight; it is the same page. The round button beside the bar switches cities (see below).

The top of every section shows the selected city as a large heading, with its region and local time beneath; an arrow marks My location. Tap the city name to open Cities. At the top right, the round arrow refreshes the weather and the gear opens Settings.

Every section keeps something out of sight that a swipe down brings in: tonight's Moon on Today, yesterday on the Week, Activities on the Briefing and the tiles on Details. A swipe up puts it away again, and so does a tap on the section you are on in the bar. No page has a button for it.

From version 1.8, a swipe up on a page at rest shows the page in the other temperature scale for a moment (see “Can I look at the other scale without changing my setting?”).

Twofold draws no scroll bars. A page that is longer than the screen still scrolls, and stops at its ends without a bounce. No page refreshes on a pull (see the next question).

How do I refresh the weather?

Tap the round arrow at the top right of any section, left of the gear. It turns into a spinner while the selected city refreshes. With a hardware keyboard, Command-R does the same.

Pulling a page down does not refresh it. A swipe down brings something in instead: tonight's Moon on Today, yesterday on the Week, Activities on the Briefing and the tiles on Details.

Twofold also refreshes the selected city when it comes to the foreground and when you choose a city. After a request limit that names a time within the hour, it refreshes once more by itself at that time, while the app is open on the same city (see “What does the note about the National Weather Service forecast mean?”). When a page shows saved weather, the note at its foot has its own Refresh button.

Where did the buttons on the Briefing and on tonight's Moon go?

Since version 1.6, no page has a button for what a swipe does. The small button beside the Briefing's greeting and the back button at the top left of tonight's Moon are gone. Swipe down on the Briefing for Activities and on Today for the Moon. To put either away, swipe up or tap its section in the bar: Briefing, or Today, which reads Tonight after dark. The Week and Details work the same way for yesterday's row and for the tiles.

With VoiceOver, the pages offer named actions instead: Show tonight's Moon and Show today, Show yesterday and Hide yesterday, Swipe down again on the Week, Show activities and Show the briefing, Show the tiles and Explain the readings.

Where did the Layout, Voice, Temperature and Widgets settings go?

Layout, Voice and Temperature are one page: Settings → Layout and voice. From top to bottom it holds the scales, the current weather layout, the units for wind and distance, the cards of Today and Details, and the voice.

The page no longer offers a choice between Dashboard and Reading list. The tiles always stand in columns. The list that Details opens on is a different thing: it says what the readings mean (see “What do the readings on Details mean?”).

There is no Widgets page. Widgets are added and edited on the Home Screen and the Lock Screen (see the widget questions below). If widgets cannot share weather with the app on your device, Settings → Help & feedback says so.

Today has no Weather data and sources button at its foot. The same page is Settings → Weather sources.

Where did the Card size setting go?

Since version 1.6, no setting sizes every card at once. Each card with two sizes has its own: long press the current reading, the hourly card, the Week card or a Briefing card and choose Make compact or Show details. Past week has the two sizes as well where it is a card of its own. Three tiles have two sizes too, Wind, Air quality and Pollen; the other tiles have one (see “Which tiles have Show details?”).

If you had chosen Compact before version 1.6, your cards stayed compact after the update, each as its own size. Show details opens one card. Restore defaults… gives the current reading, the hourly card, Past week and the three tiles the sizes of a fresh install (see “How do I get the original cards back?”); the Week card and the Briefing's cards keep theirs until you change them.

How do I change temperature units?

Open Settings → Layout and voice. Scales, at the top of the page, has four choices: Celsius first, Fahrenheit first, Celsius only and Fahrenheit only. Both scales stay the default. The app, the widgets and the Apple Watch app follow the choice. With one scale, wind, rain, pressure and distance are written in one unit as well (see the next question). Wind and distance can be set to kilometres or miles on their own (see “Can I have wind in miles and temperatures in Celsius?”). Missing readings are shown as unavailable.

For one large reading, choose Tap to switch under Current weather on the same page. Use the °C/°F and Actual/Feels like controls on the page, or tap the temperature to switch scales. These choices are saved independently of Scales.

Can I show only Celsius or only Fahrenheit?

Yes. Open Settings → Layout and voice and, under Scales, choose Celsius only or Fahrenheit only. Every temperature in the app, in the widgets and on the Apple Watch is then one value. The intro offers the same four choices on its first page, with the units for wind and distance under them. To have both scales again, choose Celsius first or Fahrenheit first.

Feels like takes the place where the second scale stood. The current reading always shows it there. An hour or a day shows it, as “Feels 5°”, only when feels like is 2°C (3.6°F) or more from the temperature; otherwise that place stays empty and the row keeps its height.

One scale also means one set of units. With Celsius only, wind, rain, pressure and distance read in km/h, mm, hPa and km; with Fahrenheit only, in mph, inches, inHg and miles. This holds in the app, in the widgets and the Lock Screen details, and in a forecast alert's threshold and text. With both scales they read in both units, as before. Wind and distance can be set apart from the scales (see the next question). A unit inside one of the voice's lines or a hidden place's facts is written copy and stays as written.

The current weather layouts follow. Big number sets feels like under the temperature, where the other scale stood. Side by side shows actual beside feels like, one value each. Tap to switch has no °C/°F control, and a tap on the temperature switches between actual and feels like.

Can I have wind in miles and temperatures in Celsius?

Yes, from version 1.8. Open Settings → Layout and voice. Wind and distance, under Current weather, has three choices: Follow the scales, Kilometres and Miles. Follow the scales is where every install starts: wind and distance read in both units with both scales, and in km/h and km or in mph and miles with one. Kilometres writes km/h and km whatever the scales, and Miles writes mph and miles. Under each choice the page says what it writes with your scales. The intro offers the same three choices on its first page.

The choice covers wind, gusts, visibility and other distances, such as the distance to a weather station on Weather sources. It holds in the app, in the widgets and the Lock Screen details, and in a gust alert's threshold and text. Rain and pressure always follow the scales. So Celsius only with Miles reads as forecasts in the UK do: °C, mph and miles, millimetres and hectopascals.

Can I look at the other scale without changing my setting?

Yes, from version 1.8. Swipe up on Today, the Week, the Briefing or Details while the page is at rest. The page then reads as it would for someone who uses the other scale. With both scales, the other one comes first. With one scale, the other stands alone, with its own units for rain and pressure, and for wind and distance too unless you set those apart. The line under the city's name says so, for example “°F first, for now”.

Swipe down, tap the section you are on in the bar, or open another section or city, and your own scales are back. Nothing is saved: Settings, the widgets and the Apple Watch keep your choice. The pages a card opens follow the look while it lasts.

If the page shows something a swipe down brought in (tonight's Moon, yesterday, Activities or the tiles), the first swipe up puts that away, and the next one shows the other scale. On a page that is longer than the screen, scroll to its foot and swipe up once more. With VoiceOver, the line under the city's name has the actions Show the other scale and Show your scales.

From version 1.9 the figures turn over to the other scale like a departure board, and turn back when the look ends; with Reduce Motion they fade. To turn the swipe up off, go to Settings → Layout and voice and turn off Swipe up for the other scale under Scales.

Why doesn't the Fahrenheit value match converting the Celsius one?

Each pair is rounded separately from the same exact value, so converting the rounded number in your head can land one degree away. A feels-like difference of 1.6°C, for example, shows as “2°C / 3°F” (1.6°C is 2.9°F), while converting the rounded 2°C would give 4°F. Both figures are correct to the nearest degree; neither is derived from the other's rounded value.

How do I change the layout, or the size of a card?

Settings → Layout and voice holds the choices:

  • Scales: both scales in either order, or one alone (see “How do I change temperature units?”).
  • Current weather: Big number (the default), Side by side or Tap to switch, each shown as a small picture (see the next question).
  • Wind and distance: kilometres, miles, or as the scales have them (see “Can I have wind in miles and temperatures in Celsius?”).
  • Today and Details: Cards opens the card list (see “How do I move, resize or remove cards?”).
  • Voice: Plain, Friendly, Cheeky or Roast (see “What is the line in the hourly card?”).

A card's size is set on the card. Long press the current reading, the hourly card, the Week card or a Briefing card and choose Make compact or Show details; the current reading's menu also offers its layout. Past week has the two sizes where it is a card of its own, and so do the Wind, Air quality and Pollen tiles; Cards has a size button for the hourly card, Past week and those three tiles. The other tiles have one size, and no setting sizes every card at once.

A fresh install keeps the current reading and the Briefing's timeline card compact, which keeps Today and the Briefing short. Choose Show details on one of them for the fuller card; its page can then be longer than the screen.

What do the current weather layouts look like?

Settings → Layout and voice shows a small picture of each layout under Current weather; tap one to choose it. The intro shows the same pictures on its second page. The pictures are drawn in your scales from example readings, not from your weather.

  • Big number (the default): the temperature in large figures, the other scale under it, then a feels-like line.
  • Side by side: actual beside feels like.
  • Tap to switch: one reading at a time, with flat controls on the page for Actual or Feels like and for °C or °F.

With both scales showing, Big number and Side by side show actual and feels like in both. With one scale, Big number sets feels like under the temperature (see “Can I show only Celsius or only Fahrenheit?”).

Where did the Table and Thermometer layouts go?

They were removed in version 1.8, which has three layouts. If you had chosen the Table, the current reading is now drawn as Side by side, which shows the same four values. If you had chosen the Thermometer, it is drawn as Big number. An install that used Four readings before version 1.5 shows Big number, and one that used By scale, which became the Table, shows Side by side. Choose another layout under Current weather in Settings → Layout and voice at any time.

What is on Today, and what is on Details?

Today shows the current reading, then one card: the hourly forecast, whose last row shows Yesterday's high and low and opens Past week. Official alerts appear under the current reading when there are any, and so does the next-hour line when rain or snow is close. With only that, Today is meant to fit one screen on a 6.3-inch iPhone; an alert, the next-hour line, a larger current reading or more cards make it scroll. A swipe down on Today brings in tonight's Moon (see “How do I see the Moon?”).

Details holds every other card: UV index first, then Wind, Air quality, Precipitation, Humidity, Visibility, Cloud cover and Pressure. Pollen appears only where it is forecast. Details has no current reading. It opens on one line that sums the readings up, over a list with a row for each reading (see “What do the readings on Details mean?”). A swipe down puts the tiles in the list's place. They stand in columns, two on an iPhone, each tile at its own height, so with every tile at the size it starts with their page fits one screen where there is no Pollen tile. Wind, Air quality and Pollen can be opened to show more (see “Which tiles have Show details?”). Tap a row or a tile for more about that measurement.

You decide which card is on which page (see “How do I move, resize or remove cards?”).

Where are Past week, UV index and the Moon?

Past week is the last row of the hourly card on Today. It shows yesterday's high and low; tap it for yesterday's weather and how the last seven days of forecasts did (see “How do I read Past week?”). Past week is a card of its own only on Details, or on Today when the hourly card is not there. In Cards it is listed under On Today as In the hourly card; the arrow there sends it to Details.

UV index is the first reading on Details. To have its tile on Today, swipe down on Details for the tiles, long press UV index and choose Move to Today; the arrow on its row in Cards does the same.

The Moon has no card on Today or Details. It has Today's night side to itself (see the next question).

How do I see the Moon?

Swipe down on Today. Tonight's Moon takes the place of the reading and the cards, on a night sky: a large Moon, the phase's name and how much of it is lit. A smaller Moon stands each side of it: yesterday's and tomorrow's, or the nights before and after the night you chose. The card under it holds a strip of nights, the day's moonrise and moonset, the next full moon and the next new moon, a line about the Moon in Twofold's voice (unless the voice is Plain) and a note on how it is worked out. Tap a night in the strip for that night's Moon and its moonrise and moonset. A tap on a small Moon chooses its night too, when that night is in the strip. At the default text size the page fits one screen.

The strip holds 30 nights from tonight and the seven nights before it. It opens on Tonight, with the earlier nights waiting to its left in a quieter colour. Pull the strip to the right to see them: it resists at first, then gives, with a small tap from an iPhone. A shorter pull springs back. Tonight counts the next full and new moon from today, as in “In 12 days”; any other night counts them from itself, as in “12 days later”.

Swipe up, or tap Today in the bar (it reads Tonight after dark), to go back to Today. Where Today is taller than the screen, swipe down at its top. The Moon also goes when you choose another city or section. The Moon phase widget opens the same page. With VoiceOver, today's high and low on the current reading offer Show tonight's Moon, and the Moon page offers Show today.

The page needs no forecast, because the Moon is worked out on the device (see “Are sun and moon times exact?”). A day's page (tap a day on the Week) still has a Moon card; tap it for that day's Moon with the day before and the day after.

What do the readings on Details mean?

Details opens on what they mean. One line at the top sums the readings up: “All good right now.”, “Mind the wind and the rain.” for readings to mind, or “Take care with the air.” for readings that ask for more care. Under it, one card has a row for each reading on the page, in the page's order. A row shows the reading's symbol and name, its value and a word for it, such as “Gentle breeze”, “Muggy” or “Falling fast”. The word is a badge in the reading's colour: green for a good reading, amber for one to mind, red for one to take care with, and no colour for an ordinary one. Rain that is likely but adds up to less than 1 mm (0.04 in) over 24 hours has no colour, because it barely wets the ground; likely snow stays amber.

Only a reading that needs it says more: one or two short sentences under its row, first what the reading does to you, then what it does to the weather. Muggy air can read “Sweat dries slowly, so you cool down less than the temperature suggests.” A falling barometer can read “Down 5 hPa / 0.15 inHg since this time yesterday. A steady fall often brings cloud and rain.”; with one scale it reads “Down 5 hPa” or “Down 0.15 inHg”. Tap a row for the same page as its tile.

The words and sentences are written by hand and chosen by the readings; nothing is generated. They follow public scales: the World Health Organization's sun protection guidance for UV, the Beaufort scale for wind, the US Environmental Protection Agency's activity guidance for the US AQI and CPCB's health impacts for India's National AQI, the dew point for how humid air feels, and the Met Office's bands for visibility. Pressure counts as falling or rising from a change of 3.6 hPa in three hours, or of 4 hPa since the same hour the day before. The line, the colours and the sentences are general guidance, not medical or safety advice. Air quality and pollen are still model estimates, and the card says so at its foot. A reading with no value has a row that says Unavailable and no sentences.

The line is left out when a reading on the page is an old one, as with saved weather, and it does not say “All good right now.” while an official alert is in force for the city. Where the screen has room the list draws with more air and larger names; on a small phone or at large text sizes it is taller than the screen and scrolls. If no reading on the page has a value, Details shows its tiles and no list.

Swipe down for the tiles, which take the list's place. Swipe up, or tap Details in the bar, to bring the list back. Another city or another section starts with the list too. Where the page is taller than the screen, swipe down at its top and up at its foot. With VoiceOver, the list and its rows have a Show the tiles action and every tile an Explain the readings action, and a row is read with all of its sentences.

For the forecast hour by hour, tap a row or a tile: UV index opens today's UV curve; Wind, Precipitation, Humidity, Cloud cover and Visibility each open a graph of the next 24 hours; and Pressure opens the hours around now (see “What do the pages behind the tiles show?”). The hourly card on Today has 24-hour graphs too (see “What does the hourly card show?”).

Which tiles have Show details?

Three of them, from version 1.8: Wind, Air quality and Pollen. Swipe down on Details for the tiles, long press one of the three, hold still for its menu and choose Show details; Make compact closes it again. Opened, Wind adds the direction the wind comes from and its gusts, Air quality adds PM2.5 and PM10, and Pollen adds a line for each pollen type. The three start closed, and Restore defaults… closes them again. Cards has a size button for each of them, and with VoiceOver the size is an action on the tile.

The other tiles have one size, as every tile did in versions 1.6 and 1.7, so their menus have no Show details or Make compact. Their detail is on the tile's page: tap the tile, or its row in the list. The dew point is under How it feels on the Humidity page, and the day's peak chance of rain is on today's page (tap today on the Week). The Wind, Air quality and Pollen pages hold what their opened tiles show as well: the wind's direction and gusts, PM2.5 and PM10 under Pollutants, and each pollen type.

What do the pages behind the tiles show?

Each page puts the answer first. Most open with the reading, a word for it in its colour and one plain sentence on what it means for you; Precipitation opens with when rain is likely. Below that, most pages have a graph of the hours with a key, a few facts, the voice's joke line and a note on where the numbers came from.

Where a reading has a scale, What the numbers mean lists its bands in their colours and marks the band that applies: on UV index (marked Today for the day's peak), Wind (the Beaufort scale), Humidity (by dew point, as What the dew point means), Cloud cover, Pollen (What the levels mean), Air quality, Visibility and Pressure. The long Wind and Air quality scales show the bands around yours; tap Show the whole scale for the rest. Air quality uses the US AQI's six categories, or India's National AQI in Indian cities.

Visibility, Pressure and Air quality, and the dew point on the Humidity page, also show where the reading stands on a coloured bar with a pointer. Visibility adds a graph of the next 24 hours. Pressure adds a graph of the hours around now and, where those hours show one, its trend: Falling fast, Falling, Steady, Rising or Rising fast.

What does the key on a graph mean?

The key under a graph shows each mark as the graph draws it: a solid line, a dashed line, bars or a shaded stretch, each with its name. A dotted line marked Now stands at the present hour. On the UV graph, for example, the shaded hours are when protection is worth it and a dashed line marks UV 3. Past week and Yesterday have keys too, and where there is room their graphs also write each line's name beside it. With VoiceOver the key is read aloud, as in “Key. Solid line: What happened. Dashed line: Forecast the day before.”

How do I move, resize or remove cards?

Long press a card on Today or Details and move your finger to drag it to a new place on that page; the other cards make room, and the page scrolls when you hold the card near its top or foot. Keep holding still instead, and the card's menu opens:

  • Make compact or Show details, on the hourly card, on Past week as a card of its own and on the Wind, Air quality and Pollen tiles. The other tiles have one size.
  • Move up and Move down.
  • Move to Details or Move to Today, which sends the card to the other page. A drag stays on its own page.
  • Edit cards…, which opens the card list.
  • Restore defaults…, shown only when something differs from the original pages (see the next question).
  • Remove from Today or Remove from Details.

With VoiceOver, the same choices are actions on each tile.

On Details, swipe down for the tiles first: the rows of the list open their pages and do not move.

Cards (from Edit cards…, from Settings → Layout and voice, or from the note at the foot of a page that says how many cards were removed from it) lists the cards under On Today, On Details and Removed. Drag a row by its handle to reorder it within its page, tap the arrow to send a card to the other page, tap the red button to remove a card and tap the plus to bring one back. The hourly card, Past week and the Wind, Air quality and Pollen tiles have a round size button that switches them between Detailed and Compact; a row names its size, Compact or Detailed, only when it is not the size the card starts with. While Past week is part of the hourly card it has no size of its own, and the other tiles have no size button. Changes apply at once and are kept after the app restarts. On a wide iPad, full-width cards stay on the left and every tile on the right.

If you move or remove every card from Details, the page says so and offers Edit cards.

How do I get the original cards back?

Long press any card on Today or Details and choose Restore defaults…. Twofold asks first, then puts every card back on its own page, in its original place and size, and brings removed cards back. Restore defaults is also at the foot of the Cards list, where it applies at once. The menu item appears only when something differs from the original pages.

Restore defaults does not change the scales, the current weather layout, the voice or your cities. The Week card and the Briefing's cards keep their sizes too.

What does the hourly card show?

Six symbol buttons head the card. The first shows the hours; the others swap them for a 24-hour graph of temperature, rain, wind, humidity or UV. On the temperature graph the dashed line is feels like, and a small key names it. Drag across a graph to read an hour, and choose the first button to bring the hours back.

Under the buttons sits Twofold's line of commentary, unless the voice is Plain (see “What is the line in the hourly card?”), then the hours as a strip. Each hour's icon is drawn in the sky's colours, and its first temperature sits over a short bar in a colour for that temperature, with the other scale beneath it (with one scale, see “Can I show only Celsius or only Fahrenheit?”). In the Detailed size, an hour with a rain chance of 10% or more shows it under its icon. Tap an hour for its actual and feels-like readings in your scales.

Sunrise and sunset sit in the strip at their times. When a nearby station is adjusting the next hours, Settings → Weather sources says so under Forecast adjustment; from version 1.9 the card itself no longer does.

On Today the card's last row shows Yesterday's high and low, with Past week named under it. Tap it for the Past week page.

What are the earlier hours in the hourly card?

Drag the hourly strip to the right and up to three earlier hours appear before the hour in progress, in a quieter colour, with a thin line between them and now. They are the forecast model's values for those hours, not measurements from a station. Tap one and the card says so: “An earlier hour, from the forecast model. Not a measurement.”

The strip opens past them, so you see them only when you drag. It resists at first and the nearest earlier hour peeks in; drag a little further and the strip gives, with a small tap from an iPhone, and scrolls freely. A shorter drag springs back. The strip of nights on tonight's Moon has the same catch.

How do I read Past week?

Past week has two tabs, Past 7 days and Yesterday. Both use the forecast model's values, not station readings.

Past 7 days checks each day's forecast from the day before against what happened. How the forecast did gives one word for the week's highs, Spot on, Close, A bit off or Way off, and one sentence with the average miss, such as “A day ahead, the forecast high was off by 1.2°C / 2.2°F on average.” Under it, each day has a bar: up where the forecast was too warm, down where it was too cold. The day furthest off carries its figure. The numbers behind it opens the rest: which way the highs and lows leaned, the closest day and the limits of the four words.

Spot on is an average miss up to 1°C / 1.8°F, Close up to 2°C / 3.6°F and A bit off up to 3°C / 5.4°F; more is Way off. Spot on and Close are green and Way off is amber, and each day's own miss takes the same colours. Highs and lows draws the week, and Day by day has a row for each day: its high and low, the forecast high and how far off it was. Tap a row for the forecast low, the forecast two days before and what Twofold showed.

Yesterday shows yesterday's high, low, precipitation and strongest wind, then Today versus yesterday: a headline such as “Warmer than yesterday”, how today's forecast high, forecast low and this hour differ, and a graph of today's hours over yesterday's.

What does the Week show?

The days ahead, up to ten, on one page sized to fit the screen. Each row shows the weekday and the day's low and high on one shared scale, joined by a soft ribbon. A headline at the top names what stands out, and a day can carry one tag: Frost, Wettest, Warmest or Coldest. When nothing stands out, there is no headline. Tap a day for its forecast, sunrise, sunset and daylight.

While the Week fits the screen it stands still and does not scroll. On a small phone or at large text sizes the Week is taller than the screen and scrolls. Like every page, it does not refresh on a pull; tap the round arrow at the top right.

How do I see yesterday on the Week?

Swipe down on the Week to bring in yesterday's row above today's; swipe up, or tap Week in the bar, to take it away. Where the Week is taller than the screen, swipe down at its top and up at its foot. The row also goes when you leave the page. Tap the row for Past week's Yesterday. With VoiceOver, today's row has Show yesterday and Hide yesterday actions.

To keep the row on the page, long press the Week card and turn on Always show yesterday.

Once yesterday's row is in, keep swiping down and see what Twofold's voice has to say in the headline's place (from version 1.14 in the Plain voice too, with Friendly's lines). From version 1.8 the lines come in a different order each time, and not every line comes every time. A swipe up puts it away.

What is the Briefing?

A greeting, then one card in four parts: Right now, This hour, Today and This week. Each part has up to four short lines about rain, extremes, comfort and the sky, written by hand in advance and filled in from the current readings. A line whose data is missing is left out, and a part with no lines disappears. Rain estimates and air quality are labelled as model estimates.

Activities is the Briefing's second card: Walk, Running, Cycling, Hiking, Stargazing and Drying laundry. A line at its top says what is good now and when the rest gets good, such as “Fair at best right now. Good for a walk and a run from 1 AM.” Where the air is poor now, a later time can end with “if the air clears”: the forecast has no hourly air quality, so the scores count the air for the next two hours only. Each row shows the rating now as a badge (Poor, Fair, Good or Great), what limits it (nothing is named under Great) and the best time, such as “Best 2–5 PM”. A best time that lasts a whole day reads “Best all day”, or “Best from 1 PM” when it starts later. Under each row a strip has a bar for each of the next 24 hours, its colour and height from that hour's rating. One clock under the strips and one key name the hours and the ratings. Ratings are guidance, not safety advice. The card is not on the page until you ask for it (see “Where did Activities go?”).

What are the small pictures in the Briefing?

Small drawings of what a line says, at the end of the line. Among them are the sun's arc over the day beside a sunrise, a sunset or the day's length, the Moon, the week as bars with the named day marked, the hours to midnight with their wet or dry stretch, the next hour's rain, the day's temperature curve, the temperature and feels like on one scale, a scale with a marker for UV, air quality or pressure, and the wind's arrow. A picture is drawn from the same readings as its sentence, and not every line has one.

The pictures show where the page has room: every one where the page still fits the screen with them, and on a fuller page only those that cost their sentence no extra line. Long press the card and choose Show details for up to seven lines a part instead of four, with every picture; the page can then be longer than the screen. There are no pictures at the accessibility text sizes, and VoiceOver reads the sentence, which says the same.

Where the screen has room the card also sets its lines and parts further apart.

Where did Activities go?

How do I switch, add, reorder or remove cities?

Tap the round button beside the bottom bar. It lists My location and your saved cities; tap one to show it in the same section. With no My location saved, Use my location adds it. Search cities opens Cities with the keyboard ready. A tap outside the list closes it.

How does My location work?

Add My location in Cities (or Use my location in the city list) saves a place marked with a location arrow that stays at the top of the list and counts as one of your 12 places. While Follow my location is on (under Location in Settings, on by default), each time Twofold comes to the foreground it asks iOS once for an approximate position, moves My location there if the rounded coordinates have changed, and opens on it after a launch, after 15 minutes away or after a move of 10 km or more. With the setting off, My location still moves, but the city on screen never changes. An unchanged position fetches nothing new; a new place name there only renames it.

With Precise Location on, My location is named after your neighbourhood where Apple knows one, with the city beneath; with it off, Twofold shows the city, because an approximate position can be several kilometres out. Twofold never requests location in the background, and it never asks for permission on its own: only a tap on Use my location, Add My location, Update my location or Allow location can show the permission prompt.

If access is denied or restricted, My location keeps the last known place and its saved weather, the list explains why, and Open Settings leads to the permission. Other cities are unaffected. Swipe left on My location to remove it.

Widgets know My location by its coordinates. A widget set to Follow Twofold shows it whenever it is the selected place; a widget pinned to it shows the place at those coordinates and needs a new choice in the widget editor after My location has moved to a different area.

Why has the reading not changed after a refresh?

Tap the round arrow at the top right of any section to refresh; pages do not refresh on a pull. Twofold also refreshes the selected city when returning to the foreground, and once more by itself after a request limit that names a time within the hour (see the next question). A fresh cached response may be reused, and a provider may return unchanged data. The reading's timestamp is more useful than the time you tapped refresh.

If a request fails, Twofold keeps labelled saved weather when available. Every page says so in a note at its foot, with a Refresh button. An unavailable field is not a zero reading. Air quality and National Weather Service (NWS) supplements can fail independently of the main forecast.

When a request limit holds a refresh back, the note at the foot of the page, or the message on a page without weather, says when Open-Meteo is asked again. Where that is within the hour, Twofold asks by itself at that time, up to three times in a row, while the app stays open on that city. Where it is later, the note says when Open-Meteo can be asked again; opening the app or tapping Refresh after that time asks it. Repeated refreshes before then do not bypass the limit. The local daily budget resets at midnight UTC.

What does the note about the National Weather Service forecast mean?

For a city in the US, the forecast normally comes from Open-Meteo. When Open-Meteo cannot be asked or does not answer, the National Weather Service's forecast stands in, and a small note at the foot of Today, Week, the Briefing and Details says so: “National Weather Service forecast for now.” The sentence after it says what happens next:

  • “Twofold asks Open-Meteo again in about 3 minutes.” A request limit named a time within the hour. Twofold refreshes by itself at that time if the app is still open on that city. There is nothing for you to do.
  • “Open-Meteo can be asked again in about 2 hours.” The wait is longer than an hour, or Twofold has already asked three times in a row, so it does not ask by itself. Open the app or tap Refresh after that time.
  • “Open-Meteo did not answer.” No time is known. Tap Refresh to try again.

Once the time has passed, the note is its first sentence alone until the next refresh. When Open-Meteo's forecast arrives, the note goes.

The waits come from request limits: Open-Meteo's own, or the limits Twofold keeps on each device to stay within Open-Meteo's free service. Refreshing or choosing cities many times in a short while is the usual cause. Meanwhile the National Weather Service forecast covers up to seven days, and the hours are not adjusted to a nearby station. Outside the US nothing stands in: the page keeps its saved weather where it has some, and its note ends with the same sentence about Open-Meteo. Versions before 1.8 showed a separate card about this above the cards; it is now this one note.

What do the Briefing and Details widgets show?

Why is the weather background still?

The sky moves with the current weather, such as drifting clouds, rain or snow. Motion pauses with iOS Reduce Motion, Low Power Mode, or a hidden or inactive app. Missing weather uses a neutral background.

Readings and sources

Why does the temperature differ from another app?

Open Settings → Weather sources to compare the selected city, coordinates, actual/feels-like values, provider and reading time. The page also shows a retained supporting model when a station observation is selected.

Open-Meteo supplies area-based model estimates. In the US, Twofold checks up to three nearby NWS stations and selects the newest quality-accepted observation within 25 km, subject to age and model-lag limits; elsewhere it looks for an airport report within 25 km. A station measures its own location, not every address in a city. Model estimates and station observations can disagree about temperature or clouds; Apple and Google can use different sources and update times too. Forecasts and individual detail cards keep their own source labels. Other apps may calculate feels like with a different formula; Twofold uses one for every source (see below).

Why does it feel warmer or cooler than the actual temperature?

Twofold calculates feels like the same way for every source, using the US National Weather Service convention: wind chill at or below 10°C / 50°F when there is a wind, heat index in humid heat from about 27°C / 80°F, and the actual temperature otherwise. The Briefing's Right now, today's day page (tap today on the Week) and the sources page show a short line such as “Feels 2°C / 4°F cooler because of the wind”, with the difference and the reason. It is hidden when feels like is within 1°C of the actual temperature. If the wind is missing on a cold reading, or the humidity on a hot one, feels like shows as unavailable rather than as the actual temperature.

Why is Twofold's feels like different from Apple Weather?

Mostly because the two apps define feels like differently. In humid heat Twofold uses the US National Weather Service heat index, the official US measure, which reads above the air temperature; in cold wind it uses the NWS wind chill. The current reading does not name the formula. Settings → Weather sources names it, under Feels like formula, and the Briefing's Right now says by how much feels like differs and why. Apple Weather uses its own formula, so the two feels-like numbers can differ by several degrees even when the temperatures agree.

The temperatures themselves can differ too. Near a US weather station, Twofold shows the station's measurement, taken as the median of its recent reports and checked against other nearby stations, while other apps may use a model estimate for the area, a different station or a different update time.

Why does feels like match the temperature on mild days?

Between about 10°C / 50°F and 27°C / 80°F neither wind chill nor heat index applies, so feels like is the actual temperature. Other apps may use a different apparent temperature that also counts mild breezes and sunshine, which can read a degree or two colder or warmer. Sunshine is not part of Twofold's feels like.

What does the Precipitation card show?

The large value is today's expected total from the forecast. Now: gives the latest reading when one is available; a missing reading is left out rather than shown as zero. Tap the card for graphs of the chance and the amount over the next 24 hours; on the chance graph a dashed line marks 50%, from which the page calls rain likely. The day's peak chance of precipitation is on today's page (tap today on the Week).

In the list on Details, the Precipitation row shows the same total beside a word that looks at the next 24 hours, so after a wet morning the total can stand beside Dry ahead.

Which air quality index does Twofold show?

Air quality is a model estimate from the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS) through Open-Meteo, on the US and European AQI scales, with PM2.5, PM10, ozone and nitrogen dioxide. Indian cities lead with India's National AQI, worked out on the device by the CPCB method from 24-hour averages; when CPCB's data rules are not met, the US AQI leads instead.

The Air quality page opens with the index that leads, its category in its colour and one sentence, on a coloured bar with a pointer. What the numbers mean lists the index's six categories, the three around yours first. The other indices and the pollutants wait under More detail; tap Pollutants for PM2.5, PM10, ozone and nitrogen dioxide.

Why is pollen not available?

Pollen comes from the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS) through Open-Meteo, and CAMS models pollen only over Europe. Elsewhere the Pollen card does not appear, and the pollen page says it is unavailable rather than showing zero. In Europe, a type can show Not modelled when CAMS has no value for it, and None outside its season.

Are official alerts live everywhere?

No. Official alerts come from the National Weather Service for supported US locations and territories, and from Environment and Climate Change Canada for Canadian cities. An unavailable or saved alert check does not confirm that there are no current warnings. Bulletins are shown in the app; they send a notification only with the optional official alerts setting and only when the app itself refreshes, never from widget refreshes. Consult the relevant official weather authority for current warnings.

Widgets show no bulletins and are not a warning channel. Since version 1.6 a widget is given the result of the last check of official alerts for its city: the name and the times of each alert in force, never its text. It trusts that check for two hours. Within those two hours the Briefing widget leads with the alert's line and names the issuer and the time of the check at its foot, for example “NWS check 3:05 PM”, and the Details widget does not say “All good right now.” while an alert is in force. After two hours, or without a check, a widget does not know: it shows no alert line, and it does not say “All good right now.” either. An alert issued after the last check is not seen until the next check. The app makes one when you open it, and a widget's own update can bring one too: from the National Weather Service for a US city and, from version 1.8, from Environment Canada for a Canadian city. An update can also end without its check. Of two checks, a widget keeps the newer one. Outside the US and Canada there is no check, and a widget's words follow the readings alone.

Are sun and moon times exact?

Sunrise and sunset appear in the hourly forecast at their times, and a day's detail page shows them with daylight length. Sun times come from the forecast when available. When the forecast has none, the device calculates them: the hourly forecast marks these with ≈ and the day page labels them Approximate. Terrain and atmospheric conditions limit precision. Some polar dates have no sunrise or sunset.

The Moon is always worked out on the device. From version 1.9 Settings → Weather sources says so, and the night side's card keeps only the note that the Moon is drawn as seen from the Southern Hemisphere, where it is. In the strip of nights a gold ring marks each full moon and a dotted ring each new moon. Phases and how much is lit come from the average length of the lunar month, so the date of a full or new moon can be a day out. Moonrise and moonset are worked out for a level horizon at sea level. In most of the world they are close to an almanac's times; hills, buildings and the air near the horizon can still shift what you see by a few minutes. In the far north or south, a Moon that skims the horizon can be shown rising or setting when it does not quite, or the other way round. A day with no moonrise or no moonset says None this day.

Forecast alerts

How do forecast alerts work?

Open Settings → Forecast alerts and tap Add alert. Choose temperature or feels like above or below a value, rain chance, wind gusts, or UV index. A threshold is shown in your scales and units. A temperature threshold moves by one degree Celsius, or by one degree Fahrenheit with Fahrenheit only. A gust threshold moves by 5 km/h, from 20 to 150 km/h, or by 5 mph, from 15 to 90 mph, where your wind reads in mph alone: with Fahrenheit only, or with Miles chosen under Wind and distance. Each alert can follow one saved city or all of them, and notify at the forecast hour or 1, 3 or 12 hours before. Up to 10 alerts can be saved. Swipe left on an alert to delete it. Removing a city deletes alerts set for that city only. Forecast alerts are separate from the official alerts card on Today.

Twofold looks through the next 36 hours of each city's saved hourly forecast and schedules one notification each time the forecast newly crosses your threshold. A condition already under way does not notify again, and the same onset is not repeated after a refresh. If the lead time has already passed, the notification arrives about a minute after planning.

These are your own thresholds, not official warnings. Notifications are scheduled on the device; no server sends them. Tapping a notification opens the city it is about, and a notification that fires on a later day than its forecast names the weekday in both the onset and the “Forecast updated” time.

Can Twofold notify me about official alerts?

Yes, as an opt-in. Turn on Notify me under Official weather alerts in Settings → Forecast alerts. When a refresh brings a National Weather Service alert for a saved US city, or an Environment Canada alert for a saved Canadian city, that Twofold has not shown before, it sends a local notification at once, titled with the city and event and grouped with that city's other alerts. Alerts already on screen when you turn the option on do not notify; turning it off forgets which alerts were seen.

These notifications arrive only when the app refreshes a city: there is no background fetch, and widget refreshes do not send them. They are not time-sensitive notifications and cannot replace official warning channels. Up to 20 Twofold notifications can be pending at once.

Why are my alerts not arriving?
  • Notifications are off. Twofold asks for permission when you add your first alert. If the alerts page says notifications are off, tap Open notification settings and allow them. Focus modes and notification summaries can also hold them back.
  • The app has not refreshed recently. Alerts are planned only when Twofold refreshes a city: opening the app, returning to it, selecting a city or tapping the refresh button. There is no background fetch, and widget updates do not replan alerts. A city whose saved forecast is more than 12 hours old plans nothing new, so open Twofold now and then.
  • The alert is off or the threshold was never crossed. Check the alert's On switch and threshold. A missing forecast value never counts as a crossing; for example, a forecast without UV or feels-like data cannot trigger those alerts.
  • Too many alerts. At most 20 notifications are scheduled at once, soonest first.

Your data

How do I delete stored data?

Removing a saved city removes its entry, saved snapshot, past-week history and any alerts set for that city, but does not immediately clear the separate HTTP response cache. There is no in-app cache-reset button. Deleting the app removes its local data; offloading it preserves app data. Provider logs and existing device backups are separate. Full details are in Privacy.

What is sent over the network?

Weather requests send rounded coordinates and your IP address to the providers. City searches send the text you type to Open-Meteo. Finding the name of My location contacts Apple. There are no accounts, ads or tracking. From version 1.13 the app sends anonymous usage data (which pages and features are used, never your places or your weather), which Share anonymous usage in Settings → Help & feedback turns off.
